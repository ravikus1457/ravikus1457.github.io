Wedding (Vivah Sanskar)
paragraph
A Hindu wedding is not signed, it is vowed — before Agni, the sacred fire, with the families of both sides as witnesses. The seven pheras around the fire and the seven promises that go with them are what actually make the marriage. Pandit Ji explains each step in English as he performs it, so grandparents and grandchildren alike follow what is being promised.

Navagraha Puja
paragraph
In Vedic tradition the nine grahas — the sun, the moon and seven planetary influences — shape the timing of things in a person's life. When a period feels stuck, or a horoscope shows a difficult planetary phase, this puja is performed to settle those influences. Families come for it before a big decision, after a run of bad luck, or simply once a year for peace of mind.

Satyanarayan Katha
paragraph
The Satyanarayan Katha is a gathering to thank Lord Vishnu after something good has happened — a new job, a new baby, a house, an exam passed, a recovery. The story is read aloud in five parts and everyone present listens together, which is why it works so well with children and elders in the same room. It ends with sheera prasad shared by all.

House Warming (Griha Pravesh)
paragraph
Before a family sleeps a first night in a new home, the house itself is welcomed and settled. Pandit Ji purifies the rooms, installs the kalash, and the family boils milk until it overflows — the sign of abundance entering the house. It is a warm, short ceremony that most families follow with a meal for their neighbours.

Shanti Havan and Puja
paragraph
A havan is worship through fire: offerings are given to Agni while mantras are chanted, and the smoke is understood to carry them onward. A Shanti Havan is asked for when a household wants calm restored — after illness, after conflict, after a loss, or at the start of a new venture. People often describe the room as feeling lighter afterwards.

Rot Katta Puja
paragraph
Rot Katha is a vow-keeping puja: a family that asked the Devi for something and received it comes back to complete the promise. A thick sweet rot is prepared in ghee and gur, offered, and then shared — traditionally it is eaten within the home and not carried outside. It is one of the most personal ceremonies families ask Pandit Ji for.

Mundan Puja
paragraph
Mundan is a child's first haircut, offered as a blessing for a long and healthy life. The hair a child is born with is given back, and the ceremony marks the child stepping properly into the family and the community. It is short, cheerful, and usually the whole extended family comes.

Manglik Puja
paragraph
When Mars sits in certain houses of a birth chart, the horoscope is called manglik, and families traditionally have the dosha settled before a marriage is fixed. This puja is the remedy: specific mantras and offerings made on Pandit Ji's reading of the individual chart. Because it is based on one person's kundali, no two are identical.

Horoscope Scribing
paragraph
Pandit Ji writes out a full janam kundali from a birth date, exact time and place. Families use it for name selection, for matching before a marriage, and to understand which planetary period a person is passing through. It is kept and referred back to for a lifetime, so parents often have one made for each child.
